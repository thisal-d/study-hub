# ⚡ Lecture 02: Economics for Managers — Short Note

> **🎯 In 15 Seconds:** Economics is the study of how people, firms, and societies allocate **scarce resources** (developers, servers, capital, time) to satisfy **infinite human wants**.

---

### 📊 1. Fundamental Economic Principles

| Concept | Exact Meaning | Real IT / Business Example | Strategic Insight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scarcity** | Resources are finite and limited, while human wants are unlimited. | A software firm has only 6 senior engineers but 15 client project bids. | Forces decision-makers to make trade-offs. |
| **Opportunity Cost** | The value of the **next-best alternative sacrificed** when a choice is made. | Choosing to build an iOS App means giving up the revenue from building an Android App. | Every decision has a hidden cost of what you gave up. |
| **Needs vs. Wants** | Essential survival requirements vs. non-essential lifestyle desires. | Basic database backup (Need) vs. premium customized AI avatar generator (Want). | Secure core needs before funding discretionary wants. |
| **Neoclassical Market** | Theoretical market driven by rational buyers/sellers, perfect competition, and equilibrium. | Online cloud compute auction where prices adjust instantly to buyer/seller bids. | Prices reflect perceived consumer utility and scarcity. |

---

### 📊 2. Supply & Demand Mechanics

| Dimension | The Law of Demand | The Law of Supply | Market Equilibrium |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Relationship** | **Inverse:** Price $\uparrow \rightarrow$ Quantity Demanded $\downarrow$ | **Direct:** Price $\uparrow \rightarrow$ Quantity Supplied $\uparrow$ | Point where Quantity Supplied = Quantity Demanded. |
| **Market Reaction** | Higher prices discourage consumer purchases. | Higher prices incentivize producers to supply more output. | Market clears (no excess surplus, no shortage). |
| **Curve Shape** | Downward sloping ($\searrow$) | Upward sloping ($\nearrow$) | Intersection point ($X$). |
| **Shift Triggers (Non-Price)** | • Consumer income levels<br>• Tastes, hype, and preferences<br>• Prices of substitutes/complements<br>• Future price expectations | • Cost of inputs/raw materials<br>• Technological advancements<br>• Number of sellers in market<br>• Government taxes & subsidies | A shift of either curve establishes a **new equilibrium price and quantity**. |

#### ⚖️ Disequilibrium: Surplus vs. Shortage
* **Surplus (Price Above Equilibrium):** Quantity Supplied > Quantity Demanded $\rightarrow$ Excess stock forces sellers to discount prices.
* **Shortage (Price Below Equilibrium):** Quantity Demanded > Quantity Supplied $\rightarrow$ High buyer competition bids prices upward.

### 📊 3. Price Elasticity of Demand

$$\mathbf{\text{Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)} = \frac{\% \Delta \text{ Quantity Demanded}}{\% \Delta \text{ Price}}}$$

| Elasticity Category | Price Change Impact | Practical Examples | Managerial Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Elastic Demand** ($|\text{PED}| > 1$) | Small price increase $\rightarrow$ **Huge drop** in sales. | Non-essential mobile games, luxury gadgets, software with identical free alternatives. | Avoid raising prices; compete with discounts and promotions. |
| **Inelastic Demand** ($|\text{PED}| < 1$) | Large price increase $\rightarrow$ **Minimal drop** in sales. | Mission-critical enterprise database licenses, electricity, essential medicines (insulin). | Company can raise prices to increase total revenue without losing many users. |

### 📊 4. Production Costs & Cost Curves

$$\text{Total Cost (TC)} = \text{Fixed Cost (FC)} + \text{Variable Cost (VC)}$$
$$\text{Average Total Cost (ATC)} = \frac{\text{TC}}{Q} = \text{AFC} + \text{AVC} \quad \Big\vert \quad \text{Marginal Cost (MC)} = \frac{\Delta \text{TC}}{\Delta Q}$$

| Cost Measure | Formula / Definition | IT / Software Example | Curve Shape |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fixed Cost (FC)** | Costs that do NOT change with output volume. | Office lease, core server hardware rack lease, CEO salary. | Flat horizontal line. |
| **Variable Cost (VC)** | Costs that increase directly as output increases. | AWS bandwidth per active user, third-party API token fees. | Upward sloping line. |
| **Average Fixed Cost (AFC)** | $\text{AFC} = \frac{\text{FC}}{Q}$ | Fixed server lease spread across 10,000 active users. | Continually declines as output increases. |
| **Average Total Cost (ATC)** | $\text{ATC} = \frac{\text{Total Cost}}{Q}$ | Total cost to deliver service per single registered user. | **U-shaped curve** (falls initially due to economies of scale, then rises). |
| **Marginal Cost (MC)** | $\text{MC} = \frac{\Delta \text{TC}}{\Delta Q}$ | The additional cost incurred to onboard **1 extra customer**. | Crosses the ATC curve at the **minimum point of ATC**. |

### 📊 5. Economic Surplus & Benefits Calculation

| Surplus Type | Exact Formula | Numerical Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Consumer Surplus** | $\text{Willingness to Pay (Reservation Price)} - \text{Actual Price Paid}$ | A student is willing to pay $\$50$ for an IDE, but buys it for $\$30$ $\rightarrow$ **Consumer Surplus = $\$20$**. |
| **Producer Surplus** | $\text{Price Received} - \text{Cost of Production}$ | Studio sells software for $\$30$ that cost $\$20$ to produce $\rightarrow$ **Producer Surplus = $\$10$**. |
| **Total Economic Benefit** | $\text{Consumer Surplus} + \text{Producer Surplus}$ | Total economic welfare generated = $\$20 + \$10 = \mathbf{\$30}$. |

### 📖 Jargon & Specific Words Buster (Academic Words $\rightarrow$ Plain English)

| Specific Word / Term | Precise Academic Meaning | Plain English Translation | Memory Trick |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reservation Price** | The maximum price a consumer is willing to pay for a good or service. | The highest price you’d pay before walking away. | The ceiling price reserved in your mind. |
| **Diminishing Marginal Product** | The property whereby the marginal product of an input declines as the quantity of the input increases. | Adding more workers to a fixed workspace eventually yields less extra output per worker. | Too many developers editing the same file cause merge conflicts. |
| **Marginal** | Relating to the impact of one additional unit of input or output. | "One more unit" or "the very next step". | On the margin / next step. |
| **Substitute Goods** | Two goods for which an increase in the price of one leads to an increase in the demand for the other. | Goods you swap for each other (e.g. Starbucks vs Local Cafe; Figma vs Sketch). | Replacing brand A with brand B. |
| **Complementary Goods** | Two goods for which an increase in the price of one leads to a decrease in the demand for the other. | Goods used together (e.g. Smartphone + App store apps; Gaming Console + Games). | Products that complete each other. |
| **Market Equilibrium** | A state where supply and demand are balanced and prices become stable. | The sweet spot where buyers and sellers agree on price. | Equilibrium = equal balance. |

### ⚠️ Common Exam Traps

- ❌ **Mistake:** Confusing **Movement Along a Demand Curve** with a **Shift in Demand**.
  - ✅ **Movement along curve:** Caused **STRICTLY** by a change in that product's own price.
  - ✅ **Shift of curve:** Caused by **external non-price factors** (consumer income, marketing hype, substitute product prices).
- ❌ **Mistake:** Believing that when Marginal Cost (MC) is rising, Average Total Cost (ATC) must also be rising.
  - ✅ **Reality:** As long as $\text{MC} < \text{ATC}$, the Average Total Cost is still falling! ATC only rises when $\text{MC} > \text{ATC}$.
