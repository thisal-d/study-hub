# ⚡ Lecture 06: Entrepreneurship & Innovation — Short Note

> **🎯 In 15 Seconds:** Entrepreneurship is the process of identifying market opportunities, assembling scarce resources, and taking calculated financial, operational, and career risks to create a new venture that delivers customer value and scalable growth.

---

### 📊 1. Entrepreneurship vs. Intrapreneurship

| Dimension | Entrepreneurship (ව්‍යවසායකත්වය) | Intrapreneurship (අභ්‍යන්තර ව්‍යවසායකත්වය) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Where it occurs** | Starts independently in the open market. | Inside an established corporation. |
| **Financial Risk** | Entrepreneur's personal capital and assets at risk. | Corporate employer absorbs all financial loss. |
| **Resource Access** | High friction; must pitch angel investors/VCs. | Existing corporate budgets, labs, and legal teams. |
| **Reward Structure** | Direct equity, dividends, capital gains. | Salary, performance bonuses, career promotion. |
| **IT Example** | Dulith Herath founding Kapruka from scratch. | Sony engineer Ken Kutaragi inventing the PlayStation inside Sony. |

### 📊 2. The 7 Mindset Traits of Successful Tech Entrepreneurs

| Mindset Trait | Simple Meaning | Real-World Tech Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Vision & Passion** | Clear mental picture of the future and grit to pursue it. | Elon Musk pursuing reusable orbital rockets despite 3 early launch explosions. |
| **2. Risk-Taking Propensity** | Taking *calculated* risks, not gambling blindly. | Leaving a high-paying corporate tech job after validating prototype demand. |
| **3. Opportunity Recognition** | Spotting pain points where others see ordinary inconveniences. | PickMe recognizing Sri Lankan taxi overcharging and lack of meter transparency. |
| **4. Resilience (Grit)** | Bouncing back stronger after failure and customer rejection. | Airbnb founders selling novelty cereal boxes to keep servers running when rejected by VCs. |
| **5. Resourcefulness (Bootstrapping)**| Achieving ambitious results with minimal money and scrappy tools. | Building initial backend with free tier Supabase and open-source APIs. |
| **6. Continuous Learning** | Adapting product based on customer data and rapid feedback. | Pivoting Instagram from a cluttered check-in app (Burbn) into a photo-sharing app. |
| **7. Emotional Intelligence (EQ)** | Leading diverse squads, resolving friction, and pitching investors. | Motivating exhausted developers to push through crunch sprints with empathy. |

### 📊 4. Lean Startup Methodology & The MVP

- **Traditional Model (Slow & Risky):** Spend 12 months writing code in secrecy $\to$ Launch publicly $\to$ Realize nobody wants it.
- **Lean Startup Model (Fast & Capital-Efficient):**
  $$\textbf{Build} \longrightarrow \textbf{Measure} \longrightarrow \textbf{Learn} \quad (\text{Continuous Feedback Loop})$$
- **Minimum Viable Product (MVP - අවම ප්‍රායෝගික නිෂ්පාදනය):** The most basic version of a product that allows a team to collect the maximum amount of validated customer learning with the least effort.
  - *Example:* Zappos founder taking photos of shoes in local stores and posting them online before buying any inventory.

### ⚠️ Common Exam Traps

> [!CAUTION]
> 1. **Entrepreneurs are NOT reckless gamblers:** Exams test whether entrepreneurs love extreme risk. **False!** Entrepreneurs take *calculated, mitigated risks*, systematically reducing uncertainty.
> 2. **MVP is NOT half-finished, buggy software:** An MVP must be a fully functional, usable product that solves the core problem, even if it has only one single feature.
> 3. **Feasibility Study vs. Business Plan:** A feasibility study asks *"Should we do this? Is it viable?"* (an assessment). A business plan explains *"How will we execute this?"* (a roadmap).

<details>
<summary><b>1. What is the fundamental difference between an Entrepreneur and an Intrapreneur?</b></summary>

An entrepreneur creates an independent business taking on personal financial risk and owning equity. An intrapreneur acts entrepreneurially inside an established organization using corporate resources with zero personal financial loss.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2. What are the three components of the Lean Startup feedback loop?</b></summary>

**Build** (develop an MVP) $\to$ **Measure** (collect user data and feedback) $\to$ **Learn** (decide whether to persevere or pivot).
</details>
