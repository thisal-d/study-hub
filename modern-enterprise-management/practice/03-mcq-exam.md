### 1. The fundamental accounting equation is:
- [ ] A) $\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} - \text{Equity}$
- [ ] B) $\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}$
- [ ] C) $\text{Equity} = \text{Assets} + \text{Liabilities}$
- [ ] D) $\text{Liabilities} = \text{Assets} + \text{Equity}$

<details>
<summary>👉 Click to reveal Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct Answer: B) $\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}$**

* **Explanation:** The fundamental accounting equation is the foundation of double-entry bookkeeping. Every asset owned by a business is funded either by borrowing from creditors ($\text{Liabilities}$) or by capital supplied and retained by owners ($\text{Equity}$).
</details>

---

### 2. Which financial statement provides a snapshot of a business at a specific point in time?
- [ ] A) Income Statement
- [ ] B) Cash Flow Statement
- [ ] C) Balance Sheet
- [ ] D) Statement of Comprehensive Income

<details>
<summary>👉 Click to reveal Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct Answer: C) Balance Sheet**

* **Explanation:** The Balance Sheet represents a static financial snapshot at a **specific point in time** (e.g., "As of Dec 31, 2025"). In contrast, the Income Statement and Cash Flow Statement report performance over an elapsed **duration/period of time** (e.g., a quarter or full year).
</details>

### 3. Comparing Current Assets to Current Liabilities on the Balance Sheet allows users to assess:
- [ ] A) Long-term solvency
- [ ] B) Liquidity (ability to meet short-term obligations)
- [ ] C) Gross profit margin
- [ ] D) Shareholder dividend payout

<details>
<summary>👉 Click to reveal Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Correct Answer: B) Liquidity (ability to meet short-term obligations)**

* **Explanation:** **Liquidity** measures how easily an enterprise can settle debts coming due within 12 months using its short-term assets (Cash, Accounts Receivable, Inventory). A healthy Current Ratio is typically $\ge 2:1$.
</details>
