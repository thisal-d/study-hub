## 📝 Detailed Question-by-Question Solutions & Explanations

### Question 1
- **Correct Answer:** **B) $\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Equity}$**
- **Explanation:** The fundamental accounting equation is the cornerstone of double-entry bookkeeping. Every asset owned by a business is financed either by borrowing from creditors ($\text{Liabilities}$) or by capital supplied and retained by owners ($\text{Equity}$).

---

### Question 2
- **Correct Answer:** **C) Balance Sheet**
- **Explanation:** The Balance Sheet represents a static financial snapshot at a **specific point in time** (e.g., "As of December 31, 2025"). In contrast, the Income Statement and Cash Flow Statement report performance over an elapsed **duration/period of time** (e.g., a quarter or full year).

---

### Question 3
- **Correct Answer:** **B) Liquidity (ability to meet short-term obligations)**
- **Explanation:** **Liquidity** measures how easily an enterprise can settle debts coming due within 12 months using its short-term assets (Cash, Accounts Receivable, Inventory). A healthy Current Ratio ($\text{Current Assets} / \text{Current Liabilities}$) is typically $2:1$.

---

### Question 4
- **Correct Answer:** **B) Financial Structure**
- **Explanation:** **Financial Structure** shows how the entire business is capitalized—specifically the proportion of funds raised through external borrowing/debt ($\text{Liabilities}$) versus founder equity and retained earnings ($\text{Equity}$).

---

### Question 5
- **Correct Answer:** **B) Solvency and financial risk**
- **Explanation:** When debt is substantially higher than equity (high leverage/gearing), the business carries high fixed interest commitments. If revenues decline during a market downturn, high debt creates significant default and bankruptcy risk.

---

### Question 6
- **Correct Answer:** **A) Resource Allocation**
- **Explanation:** **Resource Allocation** analyzes how effectively management deploys company capital across productive fixed assets (such as high-performance servers, AI infrastructure, and software tools) to generate revenue and support daily operations.

---

### Question 7
- **Correct Answer:** **B) Top line**
- **Explanation:** Revenue is called the **"Top Line"** because it sits at the very first line of the Income Statement, representing total gross sales before deducting any expenses or taxes.

---

### Question 8
- **Correct Answer:** **C) Net Profit / Income (Bottom line)**
- **Explanation:** Net Profit is called the **"Bottom Line"** because it is the final figure at the base of the Income Statement after subtracting Cost of Sales, Operating Expenses, Finance Costs, and Corporate Taxes.

---

### Question 9
- **Correct Answer:** **C) Patent on an AI Algorithm**
- **Explanation:** Intangible assets are identifiable, non-monetary assets without physical substance that provide long-term economic value (e.g., patents, proprietary software code, trademarks, copyright). Desks, servers, and cash are physical or financial current/fixed assets.

---

### Question 10
- **Correct Answer:** **C) 1 year**
- **Explanation:** Current assets are cash and other resources expected to be converted into cash, sold, or consumed within **one operating cycle or 12 months** (whichever is longer).

---

### Question 11
- **Correct Answer:** **C) Current Liability**
- **Explanation:** Accounts Payable represents unpaid short-term bills owed to suppliers and cloud vendors (e.g., AWS, Microsoft) that must be settled within 30 to 90 days ($< 1\text{ year}$).

---

### Question 12
- **Correct Answer:** **B) LKR 320,000**
- **Step-by-Step Calculation:**
  $$\text{Equity} = \text{Assets} - \text{Liabilities} = 500,000 - 180,000 = \mathbf{LKR\ 320,000}$$

---

### Question 13
- **Correct Answer:** **C) Long-term bank loans**
- **Explanation:** Long-term bank loans are **Liabilities** (debts owed to lenders). Shareholders' Equity includes Owner Capital Contributions, Retained Earnings, and Share Capital.

---

### Question 14
- **Correct Answer:** **B) LKR 40,000**
- **Step-by-Step Calculation:**
  $$\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Revenue} - \text{Cost of Sales} = 100,000 - 60,000 = \mathbf{LKR\ 40,000}$$

---

### Question 15
- **Correct Answer:** **A) LKR 20,000**
- **Step-by-Step Calculation:**
  $$\text{Operating Profit} = \text{Gross Profit} - \text{Operating Expenses} = 40,000 - 20,000 = \mathbf{LKR\ 20,000}$$

---

### Question 16
- **Correct Answer:** **B) $\text{Gross Profit} - \text{Operating Expenses}$**
- **Explanation:** Operating Profit (also called EBIT — Earnings Before Interest and Taxes) is computed by taking Gross Profit and deducting administrative, selling, and general operating overhead expenses.

---

### Question 17
- **Correct Answer:** **B) Cost Control**
- **Explanation:** The **Cost Control** insight evaluates whether operating expenses and production costs remain proportional to revenue growth.

---

### Question 18
- **Correct Answer:** **B) Investing Activity**
- **Explanation:** Cash spent to acquire long-term productive assets (hardware, servers, infrastructure) is a **Cash Flow from Investing Activities (CFI)**.

---

### Question 19
- **Correct Answer:** **C) Loan received from a commercial bank**
- **Explanation:** **Financing Activities (CFF)** involve raising capital from lenders or equity investors (e.g., taking a bank loan, issuing shares) and returning capital (principal repayment, paying dividends).

---

### Question 20
- **Correct Answer:** **A) LKR 30,000**
- **Step-by-Step Calculation:**
  $$\text{Operating Cash Flow} = \text{Cash Inflows} - \text{Cash Outflows} = +80,000 - 50,000 = \mathbf{+LKR\ 30,000}$$

---

### Question 21
- **Correct Answer:** **B) Cash tied up in unpaid Accounts Receivable with inability to pay immediate expenses**
- **Explanation:** A company may record high accounting revenue and net profit on credit terms. If customers delay payments while the company must immediately pay staff wages, rent, and cloud bills in cash, it will run out of liquid money and face bankruptcy.

---

### Question 22
- **Correct Answer:** **B) The business can sustainably fund operations and growth from its core activities**
- **Explanation:** Positive operating cash flow proves that customer revenues generate sufficient surplus cash to sustain daily business operations without needing continuous emergency loans.

---

### Question 23
- **Correct Answer:** **B) $\text{Current Assets} / \text{Current Liabilities}$**
- **Explanation:** The standard liquidity metric testing whether short-term assets can pay off short-term liabilities.

---

### Question 24
- **Correct Answer:** **B) The company owns LKR 2 in current assets for every LKR 1 of short-term obligations**
- **Explanation:** A Current Ratio of $2:1$ ($2.0$) indicates that short-term assets are double the short-term debts, providing a solid safety cushion against unexpected cash shortfalls.

---

### Question 25
- **Correct Answer:** **B) $(\text{Gross Profit} / \text{Revenue}) \times 100$**
- **Explanation:** Gross Profit Margin evaluates production and delivery efficiency as a percentage of top-line revenue.

---

### Question 26
- **Correct Answer:** **C) Gearing / Leverage ratio**
- **Explanation:** Gearing ratios measure the proportion of debt capital relative to equity capital to assess financial leverage and default risk.

---

### Question 27
- **Correct Answer:** **C) 2.3:1**
- **Step-by-Step Calculation:**
  $$\text{Debt-to-Equity} = \frac{\text{Total Debt}}{\text{Total Equity}} = \frac{230}{100} = \mathbf{2.3:1}$$

---

### Question 28
- **Correct Answer:** **B) Heavy upfront infrastructure capital expenditure (towers, fiber, spectrum) funded by debt**
- **Explanation:** Telecom networks require billions in capital expenditure upfront for infrastructure. Using debt enables fast rollout without massive shareholder dilution. Steady recurring subscription cash flows safely service the debt.

---

### Question 29
- **Correct Answer:** **A) Customers pay their invoices quickly, improving working capital liquidity**
- **Explanation:** Fast receivables collection (such as prepaid mobile recharges or card subscriptions) speeds up the conversion of sales into liquid cash.

---

### Question 30
- **Correct Answer:** **B) Accounts Receivable**
- **Explanation:** Accounts Receivable represents money owed by customers that will be collected in cash within 30–90 days ($< 1\text{ year}$).

---

### Question 31
- **Correct Answer:** **B) Software Developer Monthly Wages**
- **Explanation:** Salaries are consumed operational expenses. Data center buildings are fixed assets, common stock is equity, and loan principal repayments are financing cash outflows.

---

### Question 32
- **Correct Answer:** **C) Financing Activities**
- **Explanation:** Selling new shares brings equity capital into the company from owners, categorized under **Financing Activities (CFF)**.

---

### Question 33
- **Correct Answer:** **C) LKR 95,000**
- **Step-by-Step Calculation:**
  $$\text{Liabilities} = \text{Assets} - \text{Equity} = 85,000 - (-10,000) = 85,000 + 10,000 = \mathbf{LKR\ 95,000}$$

---

### Question 34
- **Correct Answer:** **B) Percentage of revenue remaining after ALL expenses, finance costs, and taxes**
- **Explanation:** Net Profit Margin ($\text{Net Profit} / \text{Revenue} \times 100$) measures the final bottom-line profitability converted from sales.

---

### Question 35
- **Correct Answer:** **B) $-15,000$**
- **Step-by-Step Calculation:**
  $$\text{Investing Cash Flow (CFI)} = +5,000 (\text{Asset Sale}) - 20,000 (\text{Asset Purchase}) = \mathbf{-LKR\ 15,000}$$
