### Question 2 (Short Answer Concept Analysis)
Define and clearly contrast the following accounting pairs from a managerial and IT perspective:
1. **Expense vs. Expenditure:**  
   Explain the difference and provide an example of an IT expenditure that is recorded as an asset investment rather than an immediate operational expense.
2. **Net Profit vs. Operating Cash Flow:**  
   Explain why an IT startup can be highly profitable on paper (Income Statement) but still go bankrupt due to cash flow problems.

<details>
<summary>👉 Click to reveal Answer & Explanation</summary>

1. **Expense vs. Expenditure:**
   * **Expense (OpEx):** Everyday operational costs consumed within the current accounting period to run the business (e.g., developer salaries, AWS hosting bills, office electricity).
   * **Expenditure (CapEx):** Any cash outflow. Purchasing a LKR 2,500,000 server rack is a **Capital Expenditure** recorded on the Balance Sheet as a **Fixed Asset**, not an immediate one-month expense.
2. **Net Profit vs. Operating Cash Flow:**
   * Accounting uses **accrual basis**, recording revenue when invoiced. If a startup invoices LKR 5,000,000 on 90-day credit, it shows a profit on paper. But if cash hasn't arrived and staff salaries must be paid in cash this Friday, the startup suffers an immediate liquidity collapse and goes **bankrupt**.
</details>

### Question 6 (Profit Levels & Formulas)
State the exact formula and practical meaning for each profit tier:
1. **Gross Profit**
2. **Operating Profit (EBIT)**
3. **Net Profit**

<details>
<summary>👉 Click to reveal Answer & Explanation</summary>

1. **Gross Profit:** $\text{Revenue} - \text{Cost of Sales (COGS)}$  
   *Meaning:* Measures core production and service delivery efficiency before overheads.
2. **Operating Profit (EBIT):** $\text{Gross Profit} - \text{Operating Expenses}$  
   *Meaning:* Measures profitability generated strictly from day-to-day core business operations.
3. **Net Profit:** $\text{Operating Profit} - (\text{Finance Costs} + \text{Taxes})$  
   *Meaning:* The final "bottom line" residual profit available to owners and shareholders.
</details>

## 📌 Part 4: Practical Cash Flow Management

### Question 7 (Why Cash Flow Matters)
State **four distinct reasons** why monitoring Cash Flow is critical for an IT enterprise.

<details>
<summary>👉 Click to reveal Answer & Explanation</summary>

1. **Ensuring Liquidity:** Guarantees sufficient cash on hand to pay monthly salaries, server bills, and vendor obligations without default.
2. **Supporting Business Survival:** Prevents insolvency; profitable businesses still collapse if cash dries up.
3. **Indicating Financial Health:** Positive operating cash flow proves the core IT business model is self-funding.
4. **Improving Decision-Making:** Helps IT leadership schedule major CapEx upgrades based on real cash availability.
</details>

<summary>👉 Click to reveal Answer & Explanation</summary>

## 📌 Part 6: Comprehensive Income Statement Calculations

### Question 11 (Multi-Tier Income Statement)
*CodeCraft Solutions PLC* reports the following figures for FY 2025:
* **Total Revenue:** LKR 800,000
* **Cost of Sales:** LKR 360,000
* **Operating Expenses:** LKR 240,000
* **Finance Costs (Interest):** LKR 30,000
* **Income Tax Expense:** LKR 42,500
* **Total Assets:** LKR 1,200,000 | **Total Liabilities:** LKR 500,000

**Calculate:**
1. Gross Profit
2. Operating Profit (EBIT)
3. Net Profit
4. Gross Profit Margin (%) & Net Profit Margin (%)
5. Shareholders' Equity

<details>
<summary>👉 Click to reveal Answer & Explanation</summary>

1. $\text{Gross Profit} = 800,000 - 360,000 = \mathbf{LKR\ 440,000}$
2. $\text{Operating Profit (EBIT)} = 440,000 - 240,000 = \mathbf{LKR\ 200,000}$
3. $\text{Net Profit} = (200,000 - 30,000) - 42,500 = \mathbf{LKR\ 127,500}$
4. $\text{Gross Profit Margin} = \left(\frac{440,000}{800,000}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{55\%}$  
   $\text{Net Profit Margin} = \left(\frac{127,500}{800,000}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{15.94\%}$
5. $\text{Equity} = 1,200,000 - 500,000 = \mathbf{LKR\ 700,000}$
</details>

## 📌 Part 7: Comprehensive Cash Flow Statement Calculations

### Question 12 (Net Cash Flow Calculations & Liquidity Evaluation)
*Vortex Systems Ltd* reports cash transactions for FY 2025:
* Cash collected from software clients: +LKR 650,000
* Cash paid for operational expenses: -LKR 380,000
* Purchase of server equipment: -LKR 180,000
* Sale of old hardware: +LKR 25,000
* Commercial bank loan received: +LKR 200,000
* Loan principal repaid: -LKR 75,000
* Dividends paid: -LKR 45,000

**Calculate:**
1. Operating Cash Flow (CFO)
2. Investing Cash Flow (CFI)
3. Financing Cash Flow (CFF)
4. Net Increase in Cash

<details>
<summary>👉 Click to reveal Answer & Explanation</summary>

1. $\text{CFO} = +650,000 - 380,000 = \mathbf{+LKR\ 270,000}$
2. $\text{CFI} = +25,000 - 180,000 = \mathbf{-LKR\ 155,000}$
3. $\text{CFF} = +200,000 - 75,000 - 45,000 = \mathbf{+LKR\ 80,000}$
4. $\text{Net Cash Flow} = +270,000 - 155,000 + 80,000 = \mathbf{+LKR\ 195,000}$
</details>

## 📌 Part 8: Financial Ratio Analysis

### Question 13 (Telecom Case Study Analysis)
*Dialog Telecom PLC* (Simplified figures in LKR Billions):
* **Revenue:** LKR 240B | **Cost of Sales:** LKR 120B | **Net Profit:** LKR 33B
* **Current Assets:** LKR 90B | **Current Liabilities:** LKR 45B
* **Total Debt:** LKR 270B | **Total Equity:** LKR 90B

**Calculate:**
1. Gross Profit Margin (%)
2. Net Profit Margin (%)
3. Current Ratio
4. Debt-to-Equity Ratio
5. Explain why telecom companies operate safely with high Debt-to-Equity ratios.

<details>
<summary>👉 Click to reveal Answer & Explanation</summary>

1. $\text{Gross Profit Margin} = \left(\frac{240 - 120}{240}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{50\%}$
2. $\text{Net Profit Margin} = \left(\frac{33}{240}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{13.75\%}$
3. $\text{Current Ratio} = \frac{90}{45} = \mathbf{2:1} \quad (2.0)$
4. $\text{Debt-to-Equity} = \frac{270}{90} = \mathbf{3.0:1}$
5. **High Gearing Justification in Telecom:** Telecom networks require massive upfront capital expenditures (towers, fiber, spectrum). Borrowing debt allows nationwide expansion without diluting equity ownership. Because subscriber demand is stable and recurring monthly cash inflows are predictable, the company can safely service this high debt burden.
</details>
