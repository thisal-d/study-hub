## Neoclassical Market

## A neoclassical market is a theoretical economic framework where prices,production,and consumption are driven by supply and demand;

Supply & Demand: Value of good/service is derived from perceived usefulness to the consumer rather than just the cost.

- Rational Behavior: Buyers and sellers act logically and consistently, making choices that optimize their outcomes.

Perfect Competition: Assumes numerous buyers and sellers, identical products, and an absence of barriers to entry or exit, preventing any single entity from dictating prices.

Market Equilibrium: If there is a surplus or shortage, the market automatically adjusts prices until supply matches demand, resulting in the most efficient allocation of scarce resources.

## Shifts in the Demand Curve

## Movement Along the Curve

Caused by a change in price only. The curve itself stays fixed - you simply move to a different point on the same line.

## Rightward Shift $ \uparrow $

Demand increases at every price level.

Causes: rising incomes, positive advertising, population growth.

## Leftward Shift $ \downarrow $

Demand decreases at every price level.

Causes: recession, cheaper substitutes, changing consumer tastes.

## Coffee vs. Tea:A Real Shift

When Starbucks raised prices by 10% in 2022, demand for tea-a substitute good-rose noticeably across several markets. Consumers responded rationally to the relative price change.

The tea demand curve shifted rightward not because tea's own price changed, but because a related good's price did. This is a non- price determinant shifting the entire curve.

## Cost of Production

## Costs

## Fixed Costs:

Remain constant regardless of whether a firm produces a large amount of output or produces nothing at all.

Associated with resources that cannot be easily adjusted in the short run.

## Variable Costs:

As a firm increases its production, variable costs increase, and when production decreases, variable costs also decrease.

Directly related to the resources used in the production process, such as labor, raw materials, and other inputs that vary with the level of production.

## Marginal cost:

> Refers to the increase in total cost that arises from producing one additional unit.

Helps firms determine how production changes affect their overall costs.

By comparing the marginal cost of additional unit with the potential benefit, firms can decide the most efficient level of production.

## Cost of Production

- Production costs are all the direct and indirect expenses a business incurs to manufacture a good or provide a service. They are divided into fixed costs (rent, insurance) and variable costs (raw materials, labor).

$$
\mathrm {C o s t P e r U n i t} = \frac {\mathrm {T o t a l F i x e d C o s t s} + \mathrm {T o t a l V a r i a b l e C o s t s}}{\mathrm {T o t a l U n i t s P r o d u c e d}}
$$

Variable Costs (scale with production volume)

- Raw materials

- Direct labor

Fixed Costs (remain steady regardless of output)

Factory rent

- Equipment maintenance/depreciation

Opportunity cost highlights the idea that every decision involves giving up an alternative, and rational decision making requires considering the value of the forgone option.

## Opportunity Cost Example

- Consider a software development company that has a limited number of developers and must decide how to allocate them.The company may choose between:

Developing a new mobile application, or

Improving an existing software product

- If the company decides to allocate its developers to create the new mobile application, it cannot simultaneously use those developers to enhance the existing product. In this case, the opportunity cost of developing the new application is the potential benefits that could have been gained from improving the existing software.

## Using AI for Cost Calculations

- AI can enhance how firms calculate and manage different types of production costs by automating data analysis, improving accuracy, and supporting decision-making.

- Cost of Production - AI can analyze large datasets (labor, materials, infrastructure) to estimate the total cost of production. It can also identify inefficiencies and suggest ways to reduce overall costs.

- Fixed Cost- AI helps track fixed costs such as salaries, rent, and infrastructure. It can allocate fixed costs across different products or projects to better understand cost distribution.

- Variable Cost - AI can monitor how costs change with output levels (e.g., cloud usage, raw materials). It predicts how variable costs will increase or decrease as productionscales.

Marginal Cost - AI can estimate the additional cost of producing one more unit by analyzing incremental changes in resources and output. This helps firms decide the optimal level of production.

## Benefits

<div style='text-align: center;'><img src='https://maas-watermark-prod-new.cn-wlcb.ufileos.com/ocr%2Fcrop%2F202609020901520160cf5f60ef457d%2Fcrop_1_1788310941721.png?UCloudPublicKey=TOKEN_6df395df-5d8c-4f69-90f8-a4fe46088958&Signature=1IFT5dIMBaVzUYh5LQi52rG5Ylc%3D&Expires=1788915741' alt='OCR图片'/></div>

Benefits refer to the gains or satisfaction (utility) that consumers or producers receive from consuming or producing goods and services.

<div style='text-align: center;'><img src='https://maas-watermark-prod-new.cn-wlcb.ufileos.com/ocr%2Fcrop%2F202609020901520160cf5f60ef457d%2Fcrop_2_1788310941727.png?UCloudPublicKey=TOKEN_6df395df-5d8c-4f69-90f8-a4fe46088958&Signature=3wuZK1ZFzD0tcgs%2BRjkgn9%2FepdQ%3D&Expires=1788915741' alt='OCR图片'/></div>

Benefits help in understanding how efficiently resources are used and how well a market satisfies the needs of society.

## Benefits Calculation

## Consumer Benefits

Consumers benefit when they are willing to pay more for a product than the actual price.

This extra value is known as consumer surplus.

Consumer Benefit = Willingness to Pay-Actual Price

## Producer Benefits

Producers benefit when they sell a product at a price higher than their cost of production.

This gain is known as producer surplus.

Producer Benefit = Price Received - Cost of Production

## Total Economic Benefit

Total benefit in a market is the sum of consumer surplus and producer surplus.

It reflects the overall welfare or efficiency of the market.

## Benefits Calculation Example

## Consumer Side:

A student is willing to pay $50 for software but buys it for $30

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathrm {C o n s u m e r B e n e f i t} = \mathrm {W i l l i n g n e s s t o P a y} - \mathrm {A c t u a l P r i c e} \\ = \$ 50 - \$ 30 = \underline {{\$ 20}} \\ \end{array}
$$

## Producer Side:

A firm produces a product at a cost of $20 and sells it for $30

$$
\begin{array}{l} \mathrm {P r o d u c e r B e n e f i t} = \mathrm {P r i c e R e c e i v e d} - \mathrm {C o s t o f P r o d u c t i o n} \\ = \$ 30 - \$ 20 = \underline {{\$ 10}} \\ \end{array}
$$
