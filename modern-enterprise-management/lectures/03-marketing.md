## What is Marketing?

A human-centric and technology-enabled process of creating, communicating, delivering and exchanging value to customers while building meaningful and long-term relationships.

- Meeting customer needs and wants.

- Building long-term customer relationships.

- Creation of value for customers and capturing value in return.

## Marketing vs Selling

- Selling is the immediate, transactional process of persuading a specificcustomer to buy the product

- Marketing focuses on identifying consumer needs and building long-term brand loyalty. It is a strategic process involving market research, advertising, and product development.

# Marketing Strategy

</div>

An effective marketing strategy maps, high-level goals down to tactical activities. It ensures that every marketing campaign, budget allocation, and asset created directly supports the long-term vision of the company.

## E.g.

- Vision: "Become the safest cloud-storage platform for global finance firms"

- Strategy: "Position our brand around military- grade encryption and target mid-sized banks"

- Target Market: "Chief Information Security Officers at fintech companies with 50-500 employees"

- Execution: "Publish weekly whitepapers, run highly targeted LinkedIn ads, and attend regional banking cyber security expos"

## Marketing Mix

To build marketing strategy, businesses focus on aligning the marketing mix:

- Product: What you are selling and why it is valuable to the consumer.

- Price: Your pricing strategy in relation to market demand and competitors.

- Place: Where your product is being sold or distributed.

- Promotion: How you craft the message and which channels you use to reach your target audience.

## STP Model

- The STP Model (Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning) is a key marketing strategy framework.

- It helps businesses divide a broad market into smaller groups, select the most valuable groups to focus on, and create a clear, appealing message in the minds of those specific customers

## Market Research

- Market research is the process of gathering, analyzing, and interpreting information, typically about a target audience, market, or competitors. This information helps organizations understand what people need, want, think, and do—and why.

- Use of Primary and Secondary Data Market research collects primary data (surveys and interviews) and secondary data (reports and databases) to understand customer needs, preferences, and behavior accurately.

Data-Driven Decision Making

The insights gained from research help businesses make informed, customer-focused decisions, reduce uncertainty, and improve the effectiveness of marketing strategies.

## Market Research with Digital Tools & AI

## Digital research tools are online technologies used to collect and analyze customer data.

- Include tools like web analytics, social media insights, and online surveys.

- Help track customer behavior, preferences, and interactions in real time.

- Provide accurate and large-scale data for better decision-making.

## Artificial Intelligence (AI) enhances market research by analyzing

## large datasets and predicting customer behavior.

- Identifies patterns and trends quickly.

- Enables predictive analytics and forecasting.

- Supports personalization and targeted marketing strategies.

## Product Management

## Process of guiding a product from an initial idea to its final release and beyond.

- Does the customer need this? (User Experience)

- Can we build this? (Technology)

- Does this make sense for the business? (Business Strategy)

- Focuses on designing and developing products that effectively address real customer needs and provide meaningful value.

- Decisions are guided by customer data, feedback, and usage patterns to continuously improve product performance and relevance.

- Advanced technologies and innovative features are incorporated to enhance product functionality, usability, and user experience.

- Products are developed and improved through continuous testing, feedback, and updates using approaches such as Agile and Minimum Viable Product (MVP).

## Product Life Cycle

The Product Life Cycle (PLC) describes the stages a product goes through from its introduction to its decline in the market.

## Stages of the Product Life Cycle

1. Introduction: Product is launched and awareness is created.

2. Growth: Sales increase and the product gains popularity.

3. Maturity: Market becomes saturated and competition is high.

4. Decline: Sales decrease as demand reduces or new alternatives emerge.

## Products in the IT Industry

## IT as an Enabler of Marketing 6.0

- Data Collection and Analysis - Collect customer data from websites, mobile apps and social media and run advanced analytics

- Automation - Predict customer needs, recommend products. Chatbots provide instant customer support.

- Personalization - Tailored content, offers and recommendations. Customers receive unique experiences.

- Integration - Connects multiple platforms such as websites, mobile apps, and physical stores. Customers experience consistent interactions across channels.
