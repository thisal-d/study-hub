## Key Components of Performance Management

## Performance Planning

Setting clear goals, expectations, and performance standards aligned with organizational objectives.

## Performance Measurement

Assessing employee performance using key performance indicators (KPIs) and agreed criteria.

## Feedback and Communication

Providing continuous feedback and maintaining open communication between managers and employees.

## Performance Review (Appraisal)

Periodically evaluating performance to identify achievements and areas for improvement.

## Employee Development

Identifying skill gaps and supporting training and development for future performance.

## Reward and Recognition

Linking performance outcomes to rewards to motivate and retain employees.

## Agile Team Leadership vs Traditional Management

## Agile Team Leader (Scrum Master)

Focuses on facilitating the team rather than controlling itencourages collaboration, removes obstacles, and supports team members to self-manage.

Example: In a sprint, the leader helps developers resolve blockers quickly and encourages daily stand-up discussions.

## Traditional Manager

Focuses on planning, directing, and controlling work—makes decisions and assigns tasks to team members.

Example: Assigns tasks to developers, monitors progress closely, and evaluates performance based on deadlines and output.

## Case Study Example: IFS Sri Lanka

IFS Sri Lanka, a well-known global enterprise software company with a strong presence in Sri Lanka, has faced challenges common in the local IT industry during periods of rapid change and global uncertainty.

## Realistic Problems Observed:

## High Employee Turnover

Skilled developers and engineers left for overseas opportunities or higher-paying remote jobs, especially after global remote work increased.

## Retention Challenges Due to Global Competition

International companies hiring remotely created strong competition for local talent.

## Employee Burnout and Low Motivation

High workload and tight project deadlines led to stress and reduced engagement among employees.

## Adapting to New Technologies

Rapid shifts toward cloud, AI, and new platforms required continuous reskilling, creating pressure on employees.

## Why this is Important?

This example reflects real HR challenges where organizations must:

- Strengthen reward and retention strategies.

- Focus on employee well-being and motivation.

- Invest in learning and development

- Support employees through technological change.

## Solutions to Key Problems

## High Employee Turnover $ \rightarrow $ Strengthen Retention Strategies

Offer competitive salaries aligned with global market rates.

- Introduce retention bonuses and clear career progression paths.

Expand flexible and remote work options to compete with global employers.

## Global Talent Competition -> Enhance Employer Branding

Promote company culture, innovation, and career growth opportunities.

Strengthen university partnerships and graduate hiring programs.

Build a strong employee value proposition (EVP).

## Employee Burnout & Low Motivation -> Improve Well-being & Rewards

- Introduce flexible workloads and better work-life balance policies.

Provide recognition programs and performance-based incentives.

Offer wellness programs (mental health support, leave policies).

## Solutions to Key Problems

## Adapting to New Technologies -> Focus on Learning & Development

Provide continuous training in cloud, AI, and emerging technologies.

Support certifications and career development plans.

- Allocate time for learning and innovation (similar to "innovation time").

## Overall HRM Improvement -> Integrated HR Strategy

Strengthen performance management systems with regular feedback.

- Improve communication between management and teams.

Align HR practices with long-term business and technology strategy.

## Summary

## HRM aligns people with business goals

HRM ensures that employees' skills, performance, and behaviors are aligned with organizational objectives, enabling the company to achieve strategic success through effective use of its human resources.

## Recruitment + Performance + Rewards = Core HR

Effective HRM is built on attracting the right talent, continuously managing and improving performance, and rewarding employees fairly to maintain motivation and retention.

## Leadership & Motivation drive success

Strong leadership provides direction and inspiration, while motivation ensures employees remain engaged and committed, leading to higher productivity and better team performance.

## HR is critical in IT industry success

In the IT industry, where knowledge and skills are key assets, effective HRM practices are essential for innovation, talent retention, teamwork, and overall organizational competitiveness.
