### Solution 2
1. **Revenue vs. Income:**
   - **Revenue ("Top Line"):** The gross total money earned from selling goods or services before subtracting any expenses or taxes.
   - **Income ("Bottom Line" / Net Profit):** The net earnings remaining after subtracting all production costs, operational expenses, interest, and taxes from revenue.
2. **Expense vs. Expenditure:**
   - **Expense:** Operational costs consumed in day-to-day business operations during the current accounting period (e.g., monthly server rental, staff salaries).
   - **Expenditure:** Any outflow of money. Buying long-term physical assets (e.g., purchasing 15 database servers for LKR 3,000,000) is a capital expenditure (CapEx) that creates a long-term Asset rather than an immediate operational expense.
3. **Net Profit vs. Operating Cash Flow:**
   - Profit is recorded using accrual accounting when earned, regardless of whether cash has been received. If the startup delivers LKR 5,000,000 worth of software on credit (Accounts Receivable) and clients delay payment for 90 days, the company shows a profit on paper but may have zero cash to pay developer salaries, rent, and utility bills, leading to bankruptcy.

### Solution 3
* **1 $\rightarrow$ C:** Balance Sheet $\rightarrow$ Static snapshot of assets, liabilities, and equity at a specific date.
* **2 $\rightarrow$ B:** Income Statement (P&L) $\rightarrow$ Measures profitability over a specified accounting period.
* **3 $\rightarrow$ A:** Cash Flow Statement $\rightarrow$ Details cash inflows and outflows across operating, investing, and financing activities.

## 📌 Part 3: Income Statement Solutions

### Solution 6
1. **Gross Profit:**
   $$\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Revenue} - \text{Cost of Sales}$$
   *Evaluates production/service delivery efficiency before overhead costs.*
2. **Operating Profit (EBIT):**
   $$\text{Operating Profit} = \text{Gross Profit} - \text{Operating Expenses}$$
   *Evaluates performance of core business operations.*
3. **Net Profit:**
   $$\text{Net Profit} = \text{Operating Profit} - (\text{Finance Costs} + \text{Taxes})$$
   *Final residual profit available to the business owners and shareholders.*

## 📌 Part 5: Financial Ratios Solutions

### Solution 10 (Dialog Axiata Style Analysis)
1. **Gross Profit Margin:**
   $$\text{Gross Profit} = 240 - 120 = \text{LKR } 120\text{ Billion}$$
   $$\text{Gross Profit Margin} = \left(\frac{120}{240}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{50\%}$$
2. **Net Profit Margin:**
   $$\text{Net Profit Margin} = \left(\frac{33}{240}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{13.75\%}$$
3. **Current Ratio:**
   $$\text{Current Ratio} = \frac{90}{45} = \mathbf{2:1} \quad (2.0)$$
   *(Healthy short-term liquidity; holds LKR 2 in liquid assets for every LKR 1 in current debts).*
4. **Debt-to-Equity Ratio:**
   $$\text{Debt-to-Equity} = \frac{270}{90} = \mathbf{3.0:1}$$
5. **Analytical Explanation:**
   - **Gearing Level:** A Debt-to-Equity ratio of 3.0:1 indicates **high financial gearing (high leverage)**.
   - **Industry Justification:** Telecom and digital infrastructure companies require massive initial capital expenditure (cell towers, fiber optic cables, subsea cables, data center facilities). 
   - Debt financing allows rapid nationwide network rollout without massively diluting ownership equity. 
   - Because telecom businesses enjoy highly predictable, recurring cash inflows from prepaid and postpaid subscriber bases, they can comfortably service high interest and debt obligations.
